## 검증자가 정정한 진술 (CONFIRMED/PLAUSIBLE 전체)

**cv-leakage-01** (PLAUSIBLE, major): Recordings made before change 3 (2026-11-24) used seed = base + participant number. As a result, P001 1103/1112, P002 1111/1124 (plan_changes.md:55) and P003 1120/1121/1125 (1125 run with --session 1, plan_changes.md:75) repeat the same colour-by-position schedule for each block number. build_epochs.py:38 gives the block IDs '<subj>_<date>_b<k>', so same-schedule blocks from different days become separate leave-one-block-out folds. The 'new day' leave-one-session-out analysis also tests on schedules that appear in its training set. The within-block, epoch-level permutation null (run_extra.py:59-60, perm_fine.py:12-13) gives twin blocks independent labels, so it cannot reproduce this structure. Exceptions: P003_1120_b1 has no twin, P002_1125's seed is unrecorded (probably session 2), and P08 has none. If the obs-epoch EEG carries a position-specific signal that repeats across days, accuracy and p-values would be optimistic. For the linear ERP-feature classifiers used, such a signal is probably weak, and the gray1 control under the same groups is at chance. The size of the effect cannot be estimated without raw data. It mainly weakens the borderline P003 result, not the P002 result. Fix: group CV folds by (subject, seed, block) and permute labels consistently across blocks that share a schedule.

**cv-leakage-02** (PLAUSIBLE, major): Labels are assigned per sequence. The obs1 and obs2 epochs of a sequence (6 s apart, same colour, same block) both enter the analysis (stim_rgb.py:495, build_epochs.py:29). Yet the exploratory permutation nulls shuffle labels epoch by epoch within each block (run_extra.py:59-60, perm_fine.py:12-13, rgb_pipeline.py:244-246), and run_within's binomial chance uses n = number of epochs. The planned method permutes at the sequence level (train_svm.py:65-66). Because paired epochs are correlated, the observed score varies more under H0 than the null allows, so the permutation p-values and run_within's chance95 are anti-conservative. By normal approximation (variance inflation at most 2x), P003's uncorrected p of 0.038 could rise to about 0.05-0.10, and its 'significant before correction' verdict would be lost if the within-pair correlation is above about 0.25. P002 stays significant after x19 correction (about 0.01 in the worst case). The actual inflation cannot be determined without raw data.

**cv-leakage-03** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): build_epochs.py:38 derives session and block IDs from the first four characters of the session tag and does not use the events-CSV metadata (seed, run, start_block). Two consequences follow. (1) For P003, the epoch counts (sessions.csv:9-10: 60 = block 2 + 12 sequences of block 3; 108 = blocks 3-5) show that sequences s037-s048 of block 3 enter three separate CV groups (1120_b3, 1121_b3, 1125_b3). The unapproved pre-results proposal (plan_changes.md:87) said to avoid this by dropping run-08's block 3. This is a specific case of the twin-schedule problem in cv-leakage-01. (2) The two P08 runs (1109a/1109b) are silently merged into one session namespace. That does not cause leakage, because shared group IDs stay in the same fold, but it hides run provenance. Group IDs should come from (subject, seed, block) and should be checked for repeated (seed, seq). This is minor on its own, because the bias it adds is already covered by cv-leakage-01.

**cv-leakage-04** (PLAUSIBLE, major): All 19 pipeline variants were compared on the same outer leave-one-block-out folds, with no nested selection (run_within.py:18-28). The pipeline labelled PRIMARY (ERP O1/O2 + sLDA, run_extra.py:12) is not the pre-registered ERSP + RBF-SVM model (research_context.md:91,106), and it has the highest cross-subject mean in methods.csv. The repo does not show when it was fixed, while methods.csv:11 names the 4-channel model as the baseline. If PRIMARY was chosen post hoc, its per-subject accuracies in subjects.csv (P002 44.7, which is also P002's maximum; P003 39.4) are biased upward by selection on the test folds. x19 Bonferroni covers the p-values for that choice but not the point estimates. perm_fine.py:16 also tests P003's best variant (EA + 4ch, 41.8), chosen after the fact; that test is exploratory and is not reported in the stored CSVs. None of the stored results covers the pre-registered analysis.

**cv-leakage-05** (CONFIRMED, minor): Supervised fitting stays inside the outer folds. There are two minor breaks of fold isolation. (1) Euclidean Alignment uses each session's mean covariance from all its trials, including the held-out block, before the within-subject leave-one-block-out CV (run_within.py:20; rgb_pipeline.py:71; also run_extra.py:48,70 and perm_fine.py:19). This is label-free and transductive, so little or no label information leaks. (2) The stacking meta-learner's internal cv=3 ignores blocks and sequences (rgb_pipeline.py:209), so its out-of-fold meta-features are slightly over-confident within the training fold. Neither exposes test labels. Only the F7 EA rows and the F8b stacking row are affected, and no headline conclusion depends on them.

**signal-timing-01** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): The .db loader (ba_io.load_db) concatenates the stored `time` arrays, re-sorts all samples by those stamps, and never uses the hardware Sample counter for timing. No dejitter or linear time-versus-Sample fit is applied, and Sample is not cached. Markers are mapped onto the EEG with searchsorted on these raw stamps. The pre-registered XDF path uses pyxdf, which dejitters by default, and the team's log says the Board's LSL stamps are arrival times with an interval SD of about 15.7 ms and some negative intervals. Whether the .db `time` column has the same jitter is undocumented. The stored results suggest any jitter at marker positions is mostly under 10 ms: in 7 of 10 sessions every marker passed the 10 ms nearest-sample check. If such jitter exists, it would blur early ERP features and lower accuracy. It cannot create spurious accuracy. Checking this needs the raw .db (Sample versus time).

**signal-timing-02** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): The .db pipeline filters each recording as one continuous array with zero-phase filtering. It does not segment at timestamp gaps or Sample jumps, has no per-segment reset and no startup exclusion, and cuts fixed 840-sample epochs. The only check is that a sample lies within 10 ms after the marker, so a gap later in the epoch window would go undetected. The planned XDF pipeline does all of these steps. Gap and lost-sample counts are printed but never stored, markers dropped by the edge or tolerance check are not counted, and only the first data_ table is read. The repository shows no internal gaps; the one documented dropout (P003 11-21) ends the recording, and the edge check handles that. Prevalence and impact are therefore unknown. The main effect is weaker auditability of the trial set, not a demonstrated bias.

**signal-timing-03** (CONFIRMED, minor): The ML pipeline uses zero-phase (forward-backward) 0.5-30 Hz Butterworth filtering plus a zero-phase 60 Hz notch, hard-coded, and a [-0.2, 0] s baseline. The pre-registered plan specifies a causal 1-30 Hz SOS filter with no notch, a [-1, 2] s epoch with [-1, 0] demeaning, and a [-1.8, -1.0] s ERSP baseline. The ML README documents the change, but plan_changes.md does not log it; that file only says the plan's parameters stand. Because the filter is non-causal and the baseline sits next to onset, post-onset activity can leak into the baseline. ERP onset latencies in erp_by_colour.png should therefore not be read causally. The change is label-blind and unlikely to inflate accuracy.

**signal-timing-04** (CONFIRMED, major): The ML pipeline excludes trials only when max per-channel ptp over [-0.2, 3.0] s exceeds 150 µV, or when any contact sample is 0. It omits the pre-registered criteria: abs 60 µV and ptp 120 µV on the [-1, 2] s epoch after [-1, 0] demeaning, the flat-signal check, missing-sample exclusion, the baseline-amplitude check, and the colour-skew test of rejections. The change is not logged. The files show materially different trial sets for the same recordings: P003 11-20 kept 77/180 (ML) versus 45/180 (planned QC on XDF), and P003 11-25 kept 106/108 versus 91/108. The files do not show whether 150 µV was picked from the keep-rate sweep; it is also the library default. The direction of the resulting bias is unknown. Colour-correlated artifacts are not screened, which matters most for the 4-channel models.

**signal-timing-05** (CONFIRMED, minor): Synchronisation between EEG and markers is never verified end-to-end. The 10 ms nearest-sample check cannot detect constant offsets, latency or drift. The .db clock_offset and local_clock columns and the 'sync n=' annotations are not used or cached. Rows with clock_ok==0 (marker time falls back to push time) are not filtered. Monitor lag is documented as UNVERIFIED with no photodiode measurement. synthetic_check injects into already-filtered data on the same timebase it epochs with, so it tests CV leakage only, which is all the README claims for it. Absolute ERP latencies, and possibly cross-session or Board-version comparisons, rest on unverified timing. A constant offset does not bias within-session bAcc.

**statistics-01** (CONFIRMED, critical): The pipeline labelled 'pre-specified' (run_extra.py:12) is the top-ranked of 19 pipelines compared on the same leave-one-block-out CV (methods.csv:2). The code itself treats it as selected: perm_fine.py:23 applies a x19 Bonferroni correction. It is not the pre-registered primary analysis (ERSP/Morlet features + StandardScaler + RBF SVM with nested C x gamma search and class_weight balanced; research_context.md:91-115, protocol.json:172-183). The pre-registered analysis (train_svm.py) has no stored result. Preprocessing also departs from the plan (0.5-30 Hz zero-phase + 60 Hz notch, -0.2-3.0 s epochs vs 1-30 Hz causal, no notch, -1-2 s). plan_changes.md logs none of these deviations. Consequence: the 39.6% group mean, the +5.8 pp gain over baseline and the O1/O2 > 4ch ranking are post-selection, exploratory estimates with winner's-curse optimism. Illustratively, a group t-test gives one-sided p=0.021 before and about 0.41 after x19. The per-subject p_bonf values already include a x19 within-subject selection adjustment, so P002's per-subject verdict is not invalidated by selection alone. The pre-registered RQ1/RQ2 answers are missing.

**statistics-02** (PLAUSIBLE, major): No paired or multiplicity-controlled comparison between pipelines exists. Only three pipeline×subject cells have their own permutation null. The pipeline deltas in methods.csv (mostly within +/-3.3 pp; SD of the 19 means 2.46 pp) are the same size as the single-pipeline permutation null SDs (2.6-5.9 pp per subject). Without paired tests on identical folds, none of the factor-level differences or the ranking has inferential support. Whether they exceed paired noise cannot be decided from stored files, because paired noise is smaller than the independent-sample figure used. P08's accuracies sit systematically below chance (17/19 pipelines, mean 28.3%), which merits investigation. P001's pattern (12/19, mean ~31.9%) is not clearly systematic.

**statistics-03** (CONFIRMED, minor): The Bonferroni factor (×19, perm_fine.py:23) covers only the pipeline selection within one subject. It ignores the 4 subjects and the other analyses the code ran: 18 cross-session, 6 cross-subject and 6 gray1 evaluations, plus EA permutation tests for P002 and P003. Several computed results appear in no results CSV: the EA permutation p-values, P001's cross-session results and all xDAWN generalization results. This is incomplete reporting; the reason cannot be determined. With 1000 permutations a ×76 correction cannot reach p<0.05 (P002: 0.076), and with 200 permutations p_bonf19 cannot fall below 0.0945. Normal-approximation and binomial calculations indicate P002 would survive a broader correction with more permutations, subject to the exchangeability concern in statistics-05. The P003 label is already qualified as significant only before correction. The P08/P001 verdicts follow from their uncorrected p-values, not from the permutation floor.

**statistics-04** (CONFIRMED, minor): binomial_chance (rgb_pipeline.py:214-215) assumes independent trials, a fixed classifier and plain accuracy. The analysis has same-colour obs1/obs2 pairs, fold-specific models and balanced accuracy on unbalanced classes. The stored permutation nulls are 1.14-1.29× wider than the binomial, so chance95 is anti-conservative as a significance reference. Counting sequences rather than trials would raise it further (worst case n/2: P003 threshold 40.5% > 39.4%). Impact is limited: no headline verdict uses chance95 (the per-subject verdicts use permutation p). In the gray1 control its anti-conservatism makes the control stricter, not more lenient.

**statistics-05** (PLAUSIBLE, major): The permutation nulls (run_extra.py:57-60, perm_fine.py:11-13) shuffle individual trials within each block. The true labels have more structure. Same-colour obs1/obs2 pairs exist within every sequence. Blocks from sessions run with the same session-1 seed (all three P003 sessions; at least P002 11/11 and 11/24; protocol.py:70-108, plan_changes.md:55,75) share an identical colour order and sit in separate CV groups. Trial counts imply that P003's partial block 3 from 11/21 (24 trials) duplicates block 3 of 11/25, which is the configuration plan_changes.md:87 proposed to exclude. The planned sequence-level shuffle (train_svm.py:65-71) was not used. The reported p-values may therefore be anti-conservative, P003's 0.038 most of all. The size of the bias cannot be checked without raw data. The gray1 control at or below chance argues against a large position or drift artefact. P002's margin (z≈4.6) probably survives.

**statistics-06** (CONFIRMED, major): No stored result reports the pre-registered secondary metrics (Macro-F1, MCC, confusion matrix) or any 95% CI or participant bootstrap (research_context.md:119-126, 150-151; protocol.json:184). confusion_matrix is imported but unused (rgb_pipeline.py:23), and fold_sd was computed but not carried into the results tables. Recomputed from methods.csv: the t-based 95% CI for the selected pipeline's 4-subject mean is [33.7, 45.5] and for the 4ch sLDA baseline [23.1, 44.5]. With N=4 a participant bootstrap has only 35 distinct resamples and a sign test cannot go below p=0.0625, so group-level inference is very weak. Without confusion matrices it is unknown whether the above-chance balanced accuracy comes from one class.

**statistics-07** (CONFIRMED, minor): The stored cross-subject (LOSO) accuracies are balanced accuracies pooled over all 995 trials, in which P002 has 53.6% of the weight. The per-subject held-out accuracies were only printed (run_extra.py:42). The planned statistic (train_svm.py:167-169) is the mean of per-subject BAs with a CI. No cross-session or cross-subject result has a significance test. The pooled 36.2%/35.9% are within about 0.4 pp of a naive binomial threshold (35.78%), which is itself anti-conservative. The stored results therefore do not show above-chance generalization to new people or new days.

**statistics-08** (CONFIRMED, minor): The gray1 control (run_extra.py:44-52) 'passes' by non-significance against a binomial chance95. There is no direct obs-vs-gray1 test and no equivalence bound. It uses fewer epochs than the obs analysis (115/87/60 vs about 266/121/68 expected from obs keep rates), and no file documents the gray1 rejections. At these sample sizes, confounds of the size of P003's (39.4%) or P08's (36.0%) effects would go undetected. The control argues against an artefact as large as P002's effect but is weak evidence for smaller effects.

**statistics-09** (CONFIRMED, minor): No code in the repository produces the stored results tables. The scripts write only intermediate files (results_within_*.csv, results_extra.csv, results_perm.csv, results_perm_fine.csv), so the tables were assembled by an undocumented step. The null95 shown for P002/P003 (n_perm=1000) cannot come from perm_fine.py, which computes no quantile, so its source run is unknown. perm_fine's null_sd and z were dropped. The arithmetic that can be checked (means, deltas, trial counts, binomial chance95, p×19) is internally consistent.

**prereg-repro-01** (CONFIRMED, major): The ML analysis's 'primary' model (50-ms ERP window means on O1/O2 + shrinkage LDA) is labelled 'pre-specified' (run_extra.py:12). The pre-registration fixes ERSP (Morlet, 4x4 bands/windows) + StandardScaler + RBF-SVM tuned over C={0.1,1,10}, gamma={0.01,0.1,1}, class_weight='balanced', and explicitly sets LDA/ERP aside (research_context.md:106-115). No entry in plan_changes.md logs the substitution. The only RBF-SVM in the ML code (F4b) uses ERP features, fixed C=1, default gamma and no class weighting. The planned ERSP+SVM result is not present in the repository. The 'primary' is the best-scoring of the 19 pipelines, and perm_fine applies x19 Bonferroni, which matches post-hoc selection. The P002 'significant after correction' verdict is therefore an exploratory multi-pipeline result, not an answer to the pre-registered question. It does already correct for the 19-way pipeline selection.

**prereg-repro-02** (CONFIRMED, major): The ML pipeline uses different preprocessing and QC from protocol.json, and none of it is logged in plan_changes.md. Filter: zero-phase 0.5-30 Hz + 60 Hz notch over the whole recording vs causal 1-30 Hz with per-segment reset and no notch. Epoch/baseline: -0.2-3.0 s with baseline -0.2-0 s vs -1-2 s with baseline -1.8 to -1.0 s. Rejection: PTP>150 µV + contact loss only vs abs 60 / PTP 120 / flat / baseline / missing-sample / 5-s startup. Data source: Board .db with raw timestamps vs XDF via pyxdf. Features: ERP/band-power/covariance vs ERSP. The ML code never reads the config. build_epochs.py prints keep rates at several thresholds before 150 µV is used downstream, which suggests a post-hoc, quality-driven choice. Lost samples are counted but never used, so gaps inside an epoch are not detected. The resulting trial sets differ substantially: for P003 11-20, 77/180 kept vs 45/180 under planned QC.

**prereg-repro-03** (CONFIRMED, major): P003 was assembled differently from the pending, unapproved combination proposal (plan_changes.md:81-92), and P003 classification results were produced while approval was still open. The ML data include the full 11-20 recording, which the proposal excludes (P003_1120, 77 trials kept). They include an 11-21 file whose 60 obs trials match run-08's block 2 plus the partial block 3 (s037-s048), which the proposal excludes. They include run-09 blocks 3-5 (108). They appear to omit run-05 block 1. All runs share the session-1 seed, and block IDs embed the date, so the same block-3 sequences appear in up to three separate CV groups (P003_1120_b3, P003_1121_b3, P003_1125_b3). The proposal explicitly warned against exactly this. The P003 result (39.4%, p=0.038 uncorrected, 0.721 after x19) therefore comes from a data set the documented proposal would not build. Anyone deciding the combination has now seen classification results.

**prereg-repro-04** (CONFIRMED, major): P08's v2.0 (8-phase, script 3.x) data, whose pooling with Change-1 data the team explicitly left undecided (plan_changes.md:25), is pooled into the within-subject tables, the methods.csv mean, the gray1 control and the cross-subject LOSO. By recording date and CHANGELOG.md:8, P001_1103, P001_1112 and P002_1111 were most likely also recorded under v2.0 (inference). The post-hoc participant exclusion rule is still undecided (plan_changes.md:43), yet P001 is kept at ~23% retention and P001_1119 is dropped by an ad-hoc rule. LOSO is run with exactly 4 participants, the pre-registered minimum, so it depends on these open inclusion decisions. Obs-phase structure is the same in both designs, so the bias size is uncertain and probably modest. The issue is mainly that results are provisional on undecided inclusion choices.

**prereg-repro-05** (PLAUSIBLE, major): Within-subject CV is leave-one-block-out over blocks pooled from all of a participant's sessions, not 5-fold within one visit as planned. Repeated sessions recorded before 11-24 used the same session-1 seed. As a result, a held-out block (e.g., P002_1111_b1) can have an identically ordered twin block from another day in training (P002_1124_b1; P003's three files likewise). The team explicitly deferred how repeated sessions may be used (plan_changes.md:56). Any position-within-block EEG effect could therefore be mapped to the same colour labels in train and test. The gray1 control at chance (P002 32.1%, n=115) argues against a large effect, but the size of any inflation cannot be determined without raw data. Pooling also triples P002's trial count, which increases power.

**prereg-repro-06** (CONFIRMED, major): The stored ML results report only balanced accuracy. None of the pre-registered Macro-F1, MCC, confusion matrices, participant-level bootstrap (10,000) or 95% CIs appear. Of the 19 pipelines, only F6 tunes hyperparameters by inner CV; the rest use fixed settings (apart from analytic LDA shrinkage). The method ranking is a plain 4-subject mean with no uncertainty estimate. Multiplicity correction is x19 over pipelines only. It does not cover the 4 subjects or the 9 permutation tests actually run, and at least one 1000-permutation test (P003, EA + ERP 4ch) targets that subject's best-scoring pipeline, which suggests test selection informed by results.

**prereg-repro-07** (CONFIRMED, major): The stored results cannot be regenerated from the repository. No inputs are included: Board .db files, events CSVs, session logs. No intermediates (cache/*.npz, epochs.npz) or raw outputs (results_*.csv) are included either. Scripts mix absolute /home/claude/... paths with cwd-relative 'cache/' and 'epochs.npz', and import modules from /home/claude/scripts. No code writes results/*.csv. Their formatting, Korean labels, derived columns and keep_pct rounding match hand transcription of printed stdout. The DB-to-participant mapping exists only in the hand-coded SESS table, because all DBs carry sub-001/subj-1 names. build_cache.py silently reuses existing caches.

**prereg-repro-08** (PLAUSIBLE, major): The README excludes P02's 11/03 recording because IRB/wearing was unverified. **prereg-repro-09** (CONFIRMED, minor): rgb-ml-analysis has no pinned environment. The README lists packages without versions, and pyriemann, which the code requires, appears in no requirements or lock file for this analysis. **prereg-repro-10** (CONFIRMED, minor): The module docstrings (rgb_pipeline.py:6, :11, :229) describe leave-one-session-out outer CV and session-grouped inner CV. The reported within-subject results actually use leave-one-block-out with block-grouped inner folds. Session-level LOSO applies only to run_extra's cross-session analysis. The README describes the rejection criterion as 'amplitude', but the code uses peak-to-peak over -0.2-3.0 s. The two P08 event files (1109a/1109b), which share one DB, are merged into a single session label with a shared block-number namespace. The reason for two runs is undocumented. The P08 total of 180 obs is consistent with complementary runs, and any block overlap would put both runs in the same CV group rather than leak across folds.

**confounds-quality-ethics-01** (PLAUSIBLE, major): No tracked file records an IRB approval or exemption number or date. A config note at 2026-11-03 21:02 (CHANGELOG.md:7) still describes worn-signal checks as happening 'after IRB approval', yet the analysis uses P001's main recording that began at 17:01 the same day (build_epochs.py:5; sessions.csv:2). Participants were also recorded repeatedly (3–4 visits for P001, P002 and P003), against the documented one-visit plan (research_context.md:39). The IRB-amendment checks for changes 1 and 3 are still unchecked (plan_changes.md:29,57). **confounds-quality-ethics-02** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): The stimulus colours are nominally iso-luminant with each other and with the grey background: within about 1% under an ideal sRGB display and the CIE 1931 2° observer, and within about 5% under a pure 2.2 gamma. The repository has no photometer or colorimeter measurement of the real monitor (50% brightness, 'sRGB mode'); iso-luminance verification is deferred as '별도' (CHECKLIST.md:51; stimulus-builder.md:25). No colour-vision screening is recorded. The reported above-chance decoding therefore shows that the R, G and B stimuli as displayed can be distinguished. It cannot be attributed specifically to hue as opposed to residual luminance or salience differences until photometry and screening are documented.

**confounds-quality-ethics-03** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): Before change 3 (2026-11-24) the seed depended only on the participant number, so a participant's repeated sessions used identical 90-sequence colour orders. The analysis pools such repeats: P001 1103/1112, P002 at least 1111/1124, and P003 1120/1121/1125, with P003 block 3 present three times and block 2 twice. This includes 11-20, which the team proposed excluding for repeated exposure (plan_changes.md:89). Within-subject CV groups are session-blocks, so a test block's identically ordered twin can be in training. The permutation null shuffles labels independently within each block (run_extra.py:59-60; perm_fine.py:12-13), so it does not preserve that cross-session alignment and may be somewhat anticonservative if any colour-independent, position-locked signal exists. The block constraints also make upcoming colours partly predictable: about 52% for an ideal observer versus 33% chance. Both effects are plausible but unquantified, and are unlikely to overturn P002's result (44.7 vs null95 37.1).

**confounds-quality-ethics-04** (CONFIRMED, minor): The gray1 control (run_extra.py:44-52) decodes the upcoming colour from 50–500 ms after gray1 onset, i.e. 2.95–2.5 s before the colour. It is run only for P002, P003 and P08, with 2 of 19 pipelines, and is judged against binomial chance95 without a permutation test. It can rule out gross label or block leakage (P002 32.1%, n=115). It cannot address anticipatory activity in the obs baseline window (−0.2–0 s before colour), stimulus-driven confounds (residual luminance, colour-evoked ocular responses), or effects smaller than about 9–12 pp for P003 and P08. Gray1 epochs are kept far less often than obs epochs (P002 42.6% vs 98.7%), so the control sample is a selected subset. The reason is not documented; blinks concentrated in grey periods, as the instructions would encourage, are one possibility. The control should be described as a leakage check only.

**confounds-quality-ethics-05** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): The analysis pools sessions recorded under the v2.0 8-phase/120 s-rest protocol (by date: P001_1103, P001_1112, P002_1111, P08_1109) with sessions recorded under the change-1 6-phase/30 s protocol. Going by recording dates (not otherwise verified), it also pools Board 3.0.0 and 3.1.0 recordings. The session table has no design, script or Board version field, although the team recorded the pooling decision as undecided (plan_changes.md:25). The obs epochs are structurally the same in both designs, and the documented Board differences are likely immaterial after the float32 cast. However, P002's cross-session ('new day') results confound day with design version and session length, so the drop from 44.7% to 36.0% cannot be read as day-to-day non-stationarity alone.

**confounds-quality-ethics-06** (PLAUSIBLE, major): The ML analysis replaces the pre-registered trial QC (abs 60 µV, ptp 120 µV, flat-signal and missing-sample checks; protocol.json:156-166) with a more lenient rule: 4-channel ptp < 150 µV plus contact (run_within.py:9). For P003 this keeps substantially more trials than the lab's QC (77 vs 45 on 11-20; 106 vs 91 on 11-25). Session exclusion is ad hoc: only P001_1119 is dropped, while P001 sessions keeping 22–24% stay in, and no participant-exclusion threshold has been set (plan_changes.md:43). P003 is assembled differently from the team's pending pre-results proposal (plan_changes.md:86-92): it includes 11-20 and run-08's partial block 3, and omits run-05. Data volume differs about 6-fold across subjects (83 vs 533 trials), and group means weight subjects equally. Results under the pre-registered QC and the proposed P003 assembly are unknown.

**confounds-quality-ethics-07** (PLAUSIBLE, minor): The only frontal channels are Fp1 and Fp2, which the lab's own checks treat as blink carriers. There is no EOG channel, ICA or regression, only a single 150 µV peak-to-peak rejection threshold. EA whitening uses a full 4×4 matrix, so EA variants, including 'O1·O2 + EA', mix Fp signal into the occipital features. Any 2- vs 4-channel comparison (RQ2), or any EA-based gain such as P003's EA+4ch 41.8% or new-day+EA 40.6%, therefore cannot exclude ocular contributions. The headline O1/O2 non-EA results are less exposed, and the stored data show no general 4-channel advantage.

**confounds-quality-ethics-08** (CONFIRMED, minor): Tracked files contain no direct participant identifiers, and the safeguards prohibit names. They do expose a Windows profile name ('IM') and the workstation hostname (DESKTOP-CFL7VMR), and they link pseudonymous participant IDs to exact recording timestamps and session events. In a small team that may recruit its own members, that is a modest re-identification risk. Replace these with placeholders or session indices before any public release, and confirm that consent covers sharing of derived data.
